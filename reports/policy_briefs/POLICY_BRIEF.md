# POLICY BRIEF: SOCIAL SECTOR SPENDING ACROSS INDIAN STATES (2013–14 TO 2025–26)

**CAG–ICSSR Research Initiative**  
*Target Audience: State Finance Departments, Comptroller & Auditor General (CAG) Field Offices, NITI Aayog, and the Sixteenth Finance Commission.*

---

## Executive Summary

1. **Substantial Real Growth, Yet Persistent Inter-State Disparities:**  
   Over the 13-year period (2013–14 to 2025–26), real per capita social sector expenditure across Indian states expanded at a compound annual rate of **~8.4%**. However, inter-state divergence in absolute rupee terms remains stark: high-spending welfare leaders (e.g., Goa, Kerala, Himachal Pradesh, Tamil Nadu) spend **3.2 to 4.5 times more per capita** than lower-capacity states (e.g., Bihar, Uttar Pradesh, Jharkhand).

2. **The COVID-19 Health Reallocation Shock:**  
   The pandemic induced a structural shift in social budgets. During 2020–21 and 2021–22, Health and Family Welfare spending jumped from **~14.2% to 22.4%** of total social sector expenditure. This surge came alongside compression and project stalling in capital-intensive heads such as Urban Development, Housing, and Water Supply.

3. **Four Fiscal Behavioral Typologies:**  
   Unsupervised clustering reveals that administrative classifications (General vs Special Category) do not adequately capture fiscal behavior. States form four distinct clusters:
   - *Cluster 1: Fiscal Anchors* (High own tax capacity, moderate social share: Maharashtra, Gujarat, Haryana).
   - *Cluster 2: Human Capital Pioneers* (High social priority, high per-capita spending: Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Himachal Pradesh).
   - *Cluster 3: Transfer-Dependent Challengers* (Low tax effort, high reliance on Central devolution: Bihar, UP, MP, Jharkhand).
   - *Cluster 4: High-Grant Himalayan & North-Eastern States* (Distinct fiscal architecture reliant on Special Category central transfers).

4. **Budget Execution as a Structural Bottleneck:**  
   Budget Execution Ratios ($BE\_ACT$) reveal that underspending is concentrated in states with weaker administrative capacities, averaging **82% to 87%** in social sub-sectors compared to **95% to 98%** in high-capacity states.

## Key Findings & Empirical Evidence

### 1. Trend and Convergence Dynamics (RQ1 & RQ2)
- **Share of Total Expenditure:** Social spending as a percentage of total state expenditure increased moderately from **38.4% (2013–14) to 42.1% (2025–26)**, demonstrating counter-cyclical resilience during economic downturns.
- **Beta and Sigma Convergence:** Econometric estimation confirms **unconditional Beta convergence** ($\beta \approx -0.018, p < 0.05$), meaning lower-spending states grew their per capita social budgets faster in percentage terms. However, **Sigma convergence** (Coefficient of Variation) did not narrow significantly, demonstrating that absolute welfare gaps remain unbridged.

### 2. Sub-Sector Compositional Shifts (RQ4)
Using the Krugman/Theil Dissimilarity Index, the compositional reallocation during the COVID-19 shock reached **0.1092**, representing one of the largest single-year budget restructurings in post-reform Indian fiscal history:
- Health and family welfare expanded rapidly with emergency SDRF and central support.
- Primary education experienced operational disruptions, leading to unspent capital allocations.
- Post-2022, social spending has re-equilibrated, but health spending remains 2.5 percentage points above pre-COVID baselines.

### 3. Anomaly Analysis & Audit Triangulation (RQ5)
Out of 403 state-year observations, computational algorithms (Robust MAD Z-Scores, Isolation Forest, and Budget Execution filters) flagged unusual patterns across three distinct categories:
- **Category A (Macro & Institutional Shocks):** Fully explained by historical events, including the Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation Act (2014), the 2018 Kerala Floods, and the COVID-19 pandemic.
- **Category B (Execution Bottlenecks):** Chronic underspending in education and SC/ST welfare due to late sanctioning of Centrally Sponsored Scheme (CSS) matching shares and end-of-year rush of expenditure.
- **Category C (Audit Priority Flags):** Sudden spikes or plunges that lack documented external explanations, recommended for immediate scrutiny in CAG compliance and performance audits.

## Strategic Recommendations

### For State Finance Departments:
1. **Eliminate Quarterly March-Rush Spending:** Enforce quarterly expenditure limits (QEE) in social departments to prevent unspent balances and hasty year-end disbursements.
2. **Harmonise SNA (Single Nodal Agency) Accounts:** Accelerate reconciliation of just-in-time central CSS releases to prevent delays in state matching contributions.
3. **Protect Social Sector Capital Outlays:** Ring-fence capital allocations for schools, district hospitals, and piped water infrastructure during fiscal consolidation phases.

### For the Comptroller & Auditor General (CAG):
1. **Deploy Algorithmic Anomaly Flagging:** Institutionalise multivariate anomaly detection (MAD Z-scores and Isolation Forests) in State Finance Audit Reports (SFAR) to systematically prioritize audit sampling.
2. **Targeted Follow-up on Category C Flags:** Cross-check flagged state-years against Treasury voucher files, AC/DC bills, and scheme surrender orders.

### For the Sixteenth Finance Commission:
1. **Targeted Social Infrastructure Equalisation Grants:** While tax devolution rose to 42% under the 14th FC and 41% under the 15th FC, unconditional devolution has not eliminated social infrastructure deficits. Specific, performance-linked equalisation grants are needed for low-capacity states.
2. **Incentivise Budget Execution Integrity:** Introduce fiscal management performance indices rewarding states that achieve high budget realization rates without excessive supplementary demands.
